01  ·  Multi-Agent Systems
The wiring matters more than the words
Multi-Agent Conversational Recommender System with OpenAI Agents SDK

Four AI agents collaborate to recommend movies through natural dialogue. Three generate candidate responses in parallel every turn. A fourth picks the best one. When a recommendation is rejected, a reflection layer diagnoses why — and adjusts every agent's behavior for the next turn.

The planner never writes a single word to the user. It only reasons about which response to use. Separating generation from selection is what makes failures traceable — you know immediately whether the problem was what was said or when it was said.

Two memory stores also turned out to be essential: one for what the user wants, one for how agents should behave. Mixing them breaks both.

02  ·  RAG Evaluation
You can't evaluate what you didn't build
Know Your RAG — Building an Evaluation Dataset for Public Health RAG Systems

Most RAG tutorials skip the part where you measure whether your system actually works. This post walks through building a domain-specific evaluation dataset from public health literature — LLM-generated questions, a critique filter, and RAGAS benchmarking across two pipeline types.

The paper's generic prompts did not work out of the box. Public health documents use study-scoped language, implicit population references, and biostatistics jargon that breaks standard question generation. Domain adaptation isn't optional — it's the work.

03  ·  RAG Summarization
Ask first, retrieve second
Multi-Document Summarization with RAG: Hybrid Retrieval, Debate Prompting, and SPECTER

Most RAG pipelines retrieve first, then figure out what to do with what they found. This post implements the opposite: generate targeted questions from each paper's abstract before any retrieval happens. Questions drive retrieval. Retrieved chunks answer the questions. The final agent synthesizes answers into a coherent summary.

Carrying paper provenance through the pipeline — not just text chunks — is what separates a coherent synthesis from a list of paraphrased abstracts. This is a data flow decision, not a tooling one.
